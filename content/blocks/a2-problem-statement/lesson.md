# --screen-- concept

A problem statement opens the paper and Slide 3. It makes four moves, in this order.

1. **Current situation.** How is the work done today?
2. **What exists.** What do digital systems already do?
3. **What they lack.** What gap is left?
4. **Consequence.** What goes wrong because of that gap?

# --screen-- match

## --prompt--

Match each move to the question it answers.

## --pairs--

- Current situation
  - How is the work done today?
- What exists
  - What do digital systems already do?
- What they lack
  - What gap is left?
- Consequence
  - What goes wrong because of that gap?

## --why--

A problem statement moves from today's practice to what exists, then to the gap, then to what the gap costs.

# --screen-- concept

The lecturer's example, about attendance:

> *Many institutions still rely on manual attendance methods, making it difficult to detect poor attendance and generate timely reports. While existing digital systems provide basic recording, they lack automatic identification and notification triggers when attendance falls below expected thresholds.*

# --screen-- mcq

## --question--

In the lecturer's example, which words say what existing digital systems lack?

## --options--

- [ ] "Many institutions still rely on manual attendance methods"
  > No. This is the current situation, move 1.
- [ ] "While existing digital systems provide basic recording"
  > No. This says what digital systems already do, move 2.
- [x] "they lack automatic identification and notification triggers when attendance falls below expected thresholds"
  > Yes. This is the gap, move 3. Digital systems record attendance, but nothing is triggered when it falls too low.
- [ ] "making it difficult to detect poor attendance and generate timely reports"
  > No. This says what manual methods make difficult. The gap in digital systems comes after "they lack".

## --why--

Move 2 says what digital systems already do. Move 3 says what they lack.

# --screen-- concept

The paper's Introduction (paragraph 4) lists four drawbacks of current hostel administration:

1. Room allocation is still handled at the counter.
2. Complaints are recorded without an urgency level.
3. Urgency has no deadline, and notices stay inside the application.
4. Occupancy and complaints are kept in separate records.

# --screen-- multi

## --question--

Select every drawback that the paper lists.

## --options--

- [x] Room allocation is still handled at the counter.
  > Yes. This is the first drawback. It creates queues at resumption and errors in bed-space records.
- [x] Complaints are recorded without an urgency level.
  > Yes. This is the second drawback. Urgent faults get no more attention than minor ones.
- [x] Urgency has no deadline, and notices stay inside the application.
  > Yes. This is the third drawback. Nobody is alerted when a complaint is ignored.
- [x] Occupancy and complaints are kept in separate records.
  > Yes. This is the fourth drawback. Administrators have no single view of either.
- [ ] Hostel fees are too high for most students.
  > No. The paper does not list this. Its drawbacks are about allocation, urgency, deadlines and separate records.
- [ ] Wardens are not trained to handle complaints.
  > No. The paper does not list this. The drawbacks are about how the work is organised.

## --why--

Allocation at the counter, complaints without urgency, urgency without a deadline, and separate records. These are the four drawbacks in paragraph 4 of the Introduction.
