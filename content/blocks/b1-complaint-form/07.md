# --description--

A `label` element tells the visitor what a field is for. Inside the `form`, add a `label` element with the text `What's wrong?`. Set its `for` attribute to `description`, which will be the `id` of the field you add next.

# --hints--

Your `form` element should contain a `label` element. Its opening tag is `<label>`.

```js
assert.exists(document.querySelector('form > label'));
```

Your `label` element needs a closing tag, `</label>`, with a `/` just after the `<`.

```js
assert.match(code, /<\/label\s*>/i);
```

Your `label` element should have the attribute `for="description"`. The field you add next will have the same `id`.

```js
assert.equal(document.querySelector('form > label')?.getAttribute('for'), 'description');
```

Your `label` element should have the text `What's wrong?` Check the spelling and the question mark.

```js
assert.equal(document.querySelector('form > label')?.textContent.trim(), "What's wrong?");
```

# --help--

## --hint--

The label goes inside the `form`, between its opening and closing tags. Its `for` attribute holds the `id` of the field it names.

## --hint--

The opening tag is `<label for="description">` and the closing tag is `</label>`. The text goes between them.
