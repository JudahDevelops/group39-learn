# --screen-- concept

The lecturer may ask, "Where does that come from?" Name the source and say what it showed.

First, how to read a citation in the text:

- **(Wieringa, 2014).** One author.
- **(Hevner et al., 2004).** Three or more authors.
- **(Adebanjo et al., 2026; Azeeta et al., 2021).** Two sources for one claim, in alphabetical order.

## --callout-- info

With two authors, APA 7 uses & inside brackets and "and" in a sentence, as in (Sharma & Kaur, 2021). That is an example from the course notes. It is not one of our sources.

# --screen-- mcq

## --question--

What does "et al." tell you?

## --options--

- [ ] The paper is a preprint
  > No. A preprint is marked [Preprint] in its reference, as for Heda et al. (2025). "Et al." is about the number of authors.
- [x] There are three or more authors
  > Yes. "Et al." stands in for the authors after the first, as in Hevner et al. (2004).
- [ ] The source is old
  > No. Age is shown by the year. Adebanjo et al. (2026) has "et al." and is recent.

## --why--

"Et al." means there are three or more authors.

# --screen-- concept

A reference, piece by piece. This is the Hevner et al. reference from our list:

> Hevner, A. R., March, S. T., Park, J., & Ram, S. (2004). Design science in information systems research. *MIS Quarterly, 28*(1), 75–105. https://doi.org/10.2307/25148625

Authors, year, article title, journal and volume, issue, pages, DOI.

# --screen-- match

## --prompt--

Match each piece of the Hevner et al. reference to its part.

## --pairs--

- `Hevner, A. R., March, S. T., Park, J., & Ram, S.`
  - Authors: surname, then initials
- `(2004)`
  - Year
- `Design science in information systems research`
  - Article title, in sentence case
- `MIS Quarterly, 28`
  - Journal name and volume, in italics
- `(1)`
  - Issue, in brackets
- `75–105`
  - Pages
- `https://doi.org/10.2307/25148625`
  - DOI, as a link

## --why--

Every part has a fixed place. The DOI at the end lets anyone open the paper, which is how the group checked each source.
