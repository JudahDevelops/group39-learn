# --screen-- concept

The lecturer wants every member to be able to *"articulate the problem domain without technical jargon"*. A **problem domain** is the area a problem sits in. For us, it is student hostels.

Step 1 of his 15 steps builds this skill: break the topic down, then answer in plain words.

# --screen-- concept

The lecturer gives four prompts for breaking down any topic.

| Prompt | The question it asks |
|---|---|
| Domain friction | What's the basic problem with how things are done now? |
| Affected stakeholders | Who exactly suffers from it? |
| Current flaws | What human errors, delays or fraud happen today? |
| Improvement scope | What can software realistically fix, and what can't it fix? |

## --callout-- info

A stakeholder is anyone affected by the problem.

## --why--

# --screen-- concept

The lecturer's own example is student attendance. Its first two prompts:

- **Domain friction.** Attendance is taken on paper sheets passed around the hall, then typed up later, if at all.
- **Affected stakeholders.** Students, lecturers, heads of department, the exams office

# --screen-- concept

The other two prompts for attendance:

- **Current flaws.** Students sign for absent friends; sheets get lost; nobody knows a student is below 75% until exams are near.
- **Improvement scope.** Software can record attendance, compute each student's percentage and send alerts. It can't make students come to class.

# --screen-- concept

For student hostels, the group wrote:

- **Domain friction.** Room allocation, bed-space records, check-in and check-out and maintenance complaints run on paper registers, spreadsheets and porter memory. Records sit in different hands and don't agree.
- **Affected stakeholders.** Students (allocation, complaints); porters and hall wardens (records, inspections); Student Affairs (allocation policy, occupancy); the maintenance unit (repairs); the bursary (fee clearance before allocation)

## --options--

  > Yes. The bursary marks fees as cleared, and only then can the student be allocated a room.
- [ ] It repairs rooms
  > No. Repairs belong to the maintenance unit.

  > No. The group builds the software. The bursary is a stakeholder because allocation depends on its fee clearance.

# --screen-- concept

The group's **current flaws** for hostels:

- Double or skipped allocations
- Long queues at resumption
- Complaints written in a book with no follow-up or escalation
- No live view of vacant bed spaces
- Fee clearance checked by hand

# --screen-- mcq

## --question--

"Complaints are written in a book and nobody follows up." Which prompt does this answer?

## --options--

- [ ] Affected stakeholders
  > No. That prompt asks who suffers. This sentence says what goes wrong today.
- [x] Current flaws
  > Yes. A complaint book with no follow-up is an error that happens today.
- [ ] Improvement scope
  > No. Improvement scope says what software can and can't fix. It does not list today's errors.

## --why--

Current flaws are the errors, delays and fraud that happen today. A complaint book with no follow-up is one of them.

# --screen-- concept

The group's **improvement scope**: software can digitise allocation, track bed-space occupancy, route and follow up complaints, and report to Student Affairs. It can't fix buildings or funding.

Notice the last row in both tables. Improvement scope includes what software *can't* fix. That stops a project promising things it can't build.
