# --screen-- concept

Plan about 1.5 to 2 minutes per slide.

Don't read the slide. It is for the audience. Your notes are for you.

# --screen-- mcq

## --question--

The deck has 12 slides. At 1.5 to 2 minutes per slide, how long should the whole presentation take?

## --options--

- [ ] 6 to 8 minutes
  > No. That is about half a minute per slide, far less than the plan.
- [x] 18 to 24 minutes
  > Yes. 12 slides at 1.5 minutes is 18 minutes, and at 2 minutes is 24.
- [ ] 30 to 36 minutes
  > No. That is 2.5 to 3 minutes per slide, more than the plan.

## --why--

12 slides at 1.5 to 2 minutes each is 18 to 24 minutes.

# --screen-- mcq

## --question--

A member reads every line on the slide aloud, word for word. What is wrong?

## --options--

- [x] The slide is for the audience, and your notes are for you
  > Yes. The audience can already read the slide. Your job is to say what it means.
- [ ] Nothing. Reading the slide is the safest way to present
  > No. Reading the slide adds nothing the audience cannot see for themselves.
- [ ] The member should read the deck's speaker notes aloud instead
  > No. Don't read those word for word either. Write your own speaking notes, in your own words.

## --why--

Don't read the slide. Say its point in your own words, using your own notes.

# --screen-- concept

For each slide, say three things:

- **The point.** What the slide shows.
- **The evidence.** The fact or source behind it.
- **The link.** A line that leads to the next slide.

## --callout-- info

Slide 3, Problem Statement. **Point:** hostel complaints are recorded but not managed. **Evidence:** at Babcock University students waited an average of 45 minutes for a room (Adewuyi et al., 2026). **Link:** a line that introduces Slide 4, Existing Systems.

# --screen-- mcq

## --question--

Which three things do you say for each slide?

## --options--

- [ ] The title, the bullets and the references
  > No. That reads the slide out. Say the point, the evidence and the link instead.
- [x] The point, the evidence and the link to the next slide
  > Yes. The point says what the slide shows, the evidence backs it up, and the link leads into the next slide.
- [ ] Who made the slide, when, and why
  > No. The audience needs what the slide shows, not its history.

## --why--

Point, evidence, link. Then the next speaker or the next slide follows naturally.

# --screen-- concept

Practise handovers. End your part with a line that introduces the next slide.

# --screen-- mcq

## --question--

You present Slide 4, Existing Systems, and Slide 5, Proposed Improvement, comes next. How do you end your part?

## --options--

- [ ] "That's all from me."
  > No. It stops without introducing the next slide, so the audience loses the thread.
- [x] "That is what existing systems do and don't do. Next: what the IHMS adds."
  > Yes. It closes your slide and introduces the next one.
- [ ] Say nothing, and let the next speaker start
  > No. A handover is a line that introduces the next slide. Silence leaves the audience guessing.

## --why--

A handover ends your part and introduces the next slide in one line.

# --screen-- concept

If you go blank, look at the slide title. Say its point in one plain sentence, then carry on.

# --screen-- mcq

## --question--

You go blank on Slide 10, Architecture Details. What do you do?

## --options--

- [ ] Read the whole slide aloud
  > No. Don't read the slide. The title is enough to restart you.
- [ ] Stop and wait until the words come back
  > No. Waiting leaves the audience with nothing. The slide title gives you the point.
- [x] Look at the title, say in one plain sentence that it shows what each tier does, and carry on
  > Yes. The title gives you the point. Say it plainly, then continue.

## --why--

The slide title is your way back in: say its point in one plain sentence, then carry on.

# --screen-- order

## --prompt--

Put the practice routine in order.

## --items--

- Write speaking notes in your own words, not the deck's notes word for word
- Record yourself presenting any two slides on your phone, then watch it back once
- Rehearse the whole deck as a group at least twice, with a timer

## --why--

First write your own notes. Then record two slides and watch yourself. Last, rehearse the whole deck together, twice or more, against the clock.

# --screen-- checklist

## --items--

- Share one recorded run of any two slides in the group chat.

# --screen-- flashcard

## --question--

What do you say for each slide, and what do you do if you go blank?

## --answer--

Say the point, the evidence and the link to the next slide.

If you go blank, look at the slide title, say its point in one plain sentence, then carry on.
