# --description--

A `header` element holds the top of a page. Put a `header` element around the `h1`, the `img`, the `p` and the link.

# --hints--

Your page should have a `header` element. Its opening tag is `<header>`.

```js
assert.exists(document.querySelector('header'));
```

Your `header` element needs a closing tag, `</header>`, with a `/` just after the `<`.

```js
assert.match(code, /<\/header\s*>/i);
```

Your `header` element should contain the `h1` element and the `img` element.

```js
assert.exists(document.querySelector('header > h1'));
assert.exists(document.querySelector('header > img'));
```

Your `header` element should contain the `p` element and the link.

```js
assert.exists(document.querySelector('header > p'));
assert.exists(document.querySelector('header > a'));
```

Your `h2` element and your `ul` element should stay below the `header`, not inside it.

```js
assert.notExists(document.querySelector('header h2, header ul'));
```

# --help--

## --hint--

An element can hold other elements, like a box holding smaller boxes. The `header` goes around the four elements at the top of the `body`.

## --hint--

The tags are `<header>` and `</header>`. Put the opening tag above the `h1` and the closing tag below the link.

## --hint--

Type `<header>` on the empty line above the `h1`, and `</header>` on the empty line below the link. Leave the `h2` and the `ul` outside.
